About

A Brief Introduction

I am a Master's student in the Neurocognitive Laboratory for Linguistics and Semiotics (LSCN Lab) at Sichuan University, supervised by Associate Professor Feng Gu. My research focuses on the cognitive and neural mechanisms of language processing, particularly how the brain recognizes and processes visual words, extracts meaning from linguistic input, and handles orthographic information across different writing systems.

I am proficient in experimental design and data analysis, with expertise in EEG/ERP paradigms, eye-tracking, and a range of statistical and computational tools. My goal is to advance our understanding of how the human brain achieves the remarkable feat of reading and understanding language.

Research

Research Interests

Visual Word Recognition

Investigating how the visual system processes written words, from early orthographic analysis to later lexical access, using ERP components such as N170, N250, and P3.

Semantic Processing

Exploring the neural mechanisms of meaning extraction during language comprehension, including semantic integration, prediction, and the N400 component.

Orthographic Processing

Studying how orthographic structure and regularity influence word processing, with a focus on Chinese character recognition and cross-linguistic comparisons.

EEG / ERP Methods

Designing and conducting EEG experiments to capture real-time neural responses during language processing tasks, with expertise in signal processing and component analysis.

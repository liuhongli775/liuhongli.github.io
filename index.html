Psycholinguistics · Cognitive Neuroscience · Computational Modeling

I study how linguistic knowledge is represented and processed during real-time language comprehension. I am particularly interested in the relationship between categorical linguistic representations and continuous semantic and probabilistic information, and in how experimental, neural, and computational approaches can be combined to investigate this relationship.

I am currently a Research Assistant at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University. I received my M.A. in Linguistics and Applied Linguistics and my B.A. in Chinese Language and Literature from Sichuan University.

Research Interests

My research centers on how linguistic representations emerge during comprehension and how theoretically defined linguistic categories relate to more continuous properties of linguistic experience.

Categorical and continuous linguistic representations

How much explanatory value do grammatical or categorical representations retain once expectation, semantic association, and contextual predictability are taken into account?

Computational models of human language processing

How can language models be used not merely as prediction tools, but as computational models for testing hypotheses about human comprehension?

Neural representations of language

How are linguistic and conceptual representations reflected in EEG/ERP signals, and how can univariate, multivariate, and representational analyses provide complementary evidence?

Multimodal language comprehension

How do visual and auditory information interact during real-time language processing?

Coercion or Predictability?

Mandarin Adverb–Nominal Degree Constructions

Conference poster / MA research · 2026

Do apparently construction-specific neural effects reflect categorical grammatical processing or continuous expectation-related processes?

This project combines EEG/ERP with predictors including language-model surprisal, cloze probability, and semantic association to test categorical and continuous accounts of processing difficulty.

International Symposium on Language Science (ISLS). Interdisciplinary Research and the Legacy of Yuen Ren Chao.

Methods: EEG/ERP · Language-model surprisal · Cloze probability · Semantic association · rERP

Graspability in Disguise

The cognitive and neural differences in processing words representing small and big objects

Journal article · Cortex · 2025

Which conceptual dimensions organize the neural representation of words referring to differently sized objects?

This collaborative project combines behavioral experiments, EEG, multivariate decoding, and semantic ratings to study neural differences in word meaning.

Yu, R., Liu, H., Ran, Y., & Gu, F. (2025). Graspability in disguise: The cognitive and neural differences in processing words representing small and big objects. Cortex, 187, 52–73. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2025.04.003

Methods: Behavioral experiments · EEG · MVPA · Regression RSA

ND250 as a Prediction Error Signal in Orthographic Processing

Evidence from comparing ERPs to handwritten and printed words

Journal article · Journal of Neurolinguistics · 2025

Does the 250-ms ERP difference reflect orthographic-lexicon activation or a prediction-error signal?

This project compares handwritten and printed words to investigate the functional interpretation of the ND250 during visual word recognition.

Liu, H., Zhang, J., & Gu, F. (2025). ND250 as a prediction error signal in orthographic processing: Evidence from comparing ERPs to handwritten and printed words. Journal of Neurolinguistics, 74, 101249. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneuroling.2025.101249

Methods: Experimental design · EEG/ERP · Univariate analysis · Mass-univariate analysis
